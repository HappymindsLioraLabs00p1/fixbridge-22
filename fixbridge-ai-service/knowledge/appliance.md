# Appliances — approved homeowner procedures

Source: FixBridge approved procedures, revision 1

## Appliance will not start

Before assuming a fault, rule out supply and interlocks. Most "dead appliance" callouts are a
tripped socket, a door not fully latched, or a delay timer left set.

Confirm the socket has power by plugging in something known to work, such as a lamp or a phone
charger. If that also fails, the fault is in the electrical circuit rather than the appliance, and
that is an electrician's job — do not open the appliance.

Check the door or lid is properly closed. Washing machines, dishwashers and dryers all refuse to
start unless a door interlock is made. Open the door fully and close it firmly once; a worn catch
often needs a deliberate push.

Check for a delay or child lock. A machine that lights up but does nothing is frequently waiting on
a timer, or locked. The pattern of the indicator lights identifies which — consult the manual for
that model.

Stop and call an engineer if: the appliance trips the circuit breaker when switched on, there is a
burning smell, the drum or motor is audibly seized, or water is present around the electrics.

## Washing machine will not drain

A machine that stops mid-cycle with water still in the drum usually has a blocked filter. The
filter is behind a small hatch at the front bottom of the machine and is designed to be opened by
the owner.

Switch off and unplug the machine before opening the filter. Have towels and a shallow tray ready:
the drum may hold several litres and it will come out faster than expected.

Clear the filter of coins, buttons and lint, rinse it, and refit it firmly. A filter that is not
seated squarely will leak on the next cycle.

Stop if: the drum is full and the filter is clear, the machine makes a grinding noise when
draining, or water is leaking from the body of the machine rather than the filter housing. Those
point to the pump or the drum seal, which need an engineer.

## Fridge not cooling

A fridge running but not cooling is often an airflow problem rather than a failed compressor.
Overpacking, a blocked vent, or a door seal that no longer closes will all raise the internal
temperature while the motor runs normally.

Check the door seal by closing the door on a sheet of paper. If the paper pulls out with no
resistance, the seal has failed at that point and warm air is entering continuously.

Clear the vents inside the cabinet and leave a gap around the rear of the appliance. A fridge
pushed hard against a wall cannot shed heat.

Stop if: there is ice building up at the back of the fridge compartment, the motor is running
constantly without cycling off, or there is an oily residue underneath. Those suggest a refrigerant
or compressor fault, which is sealed-system work and requires a qualified engineer.
